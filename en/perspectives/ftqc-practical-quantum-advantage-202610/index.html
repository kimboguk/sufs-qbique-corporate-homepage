Fault-tolerant Gate-based Quantum Computing

Toward Practical Quantum Advantage

Boguk Kim October 2026

Summary · Contents

Objective
  • To deliver a technical perspective on fault-tolerant quantum computing with a focus on trapped ions in plain words
  • To introduce practical quantum advantage in real application problems for potential users of quantum computing
Main theme
  • Practical quantum advantage beyond the reach of classical computation is expected to be achieved in the areas of quantum chemistry and material simulation either by pure quantum computing or through quantum-classical hybrid approaches.
  • Trapped ions have strong potential for reducing the qubit overhead by virtue of highly efficient error-correcting codes based on high gate fidelity and outstanding connectivity.

01 | What is quantum computing

Quantum computing is a new computing paradigm that processes exponentially large
computational domains utilizing the principles of quantum mechanics1.

03 | Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computing (FTQC)

Genuine quantum advantage comes from error correction.

Insight
  1. Error rates keep improving, but more efficient error correction is required to achieve practical quantum advantage.
  2. The growth rate of qubit numbers is projected to accelerate, making it possible to buy computation time with qubits.

04 | Walking cat architecture

Error correction efficiency is expected to improve further by moving ions
to connect distant qubits.

Advantage of walking cat architecture

01Lost ions replenished from separate factories transported to data nodes physically

02Simple 2D planar structure → easy to scale and modularize

  1. The cat state is the GHZ (Greenberger–Horne–Zeilinger) state or the generalized Bell state, which is maximally entangled7 in the form of (|0…000⟩ + |1…111⟩)/√2, and can be prepared with O(n) complexity.
  2. The ion shuttling time is expected to be about an order of magnitude less than the gate operation time8.
  1. 7The partial trace of the associated density matrix, leaving any single qubit, is I/2 [13].
  2. 8The ion shuttling time is assumed to be about 1/20 of the gate operation time, ignoring the effects of ion preparation and reloading.

06 | Limitations

The operation speed and readout restrict practicality.

The current status of gate operation speeds12

~ 0.2×10-3 second

~ 106 logical T-gate operations per day

Superconducting circuits [15]

Error-correction cycle time

~ 10-6 second per cycle

Classical CPU

Bit operation time

~ 10-9 second

~ 1010 bit operations per second

Readout issue

Even though entangled n-qubits can process an exponentially large amount of information, only a single n-bit outcome is retrieved upon measurement13.

Insight
  1. Presently quantum gate operations are much slower than classical counterparts, and faster gates come at the cost of lower fidelity.
  2. Quantum advantage lies in processing a large state space effectively to extract only a small amount of information, not in retrieving all the information at once, unlike in classical computing.

07 | Practical quantum advantage

Practical quantum advantage comes from application problems beyond the reach of classical computation,
solved by quantum computing alone or through quantum-classical hybrid approaches14.

Classification of application areas by practical quantum advantage

Areas where practical quantum advantage is expected

  • Breaking public-key cryptosystems based on RSA or elliptic curves through Shor’s algorithm, which shows a clear exponential advantage over classical algorithms through QFT and phase estimation [16]
  • Molecular electronic structure or binding energy problems in quantum chemistry that require formulations more general than the Heisenberg model [17], not the generalized Ising model represented in QUBO
  • Turbulent flows, other than vortices or eddies with distinct patterns, whose profiles are not effectively described by a low-order MPS15 representation and have nontrivial Fourier coefficients distributed randomly over wide ranges, when only a few core physical properties are extracted, not requiring many readouts [18]

The classical benchmarks keep rising, e.g., transformer-based quantum states for interacting electrons [22], and the advantage may be polynomial rather than exponential [17].

Areas where practical quantum advantage may exist
but trapped ions are possibly inferior to other modalities and limited due to slower operations

  • Ising problems that can be transformed into QUBO for which annealing may be more effective [19]
  • Deciphering symmetric-key systems such as AES through Grover’s algorithm featuring a quadratic advantage [20]
  • Calculation of financial portfolio risk functions with quadratic advantage

Assuming FTQC, the scope of practical quantum advantage can expand considerably as the gate operation speed becomes faster and vary depending on which modality is used.

Areas where no clear practical quantum advantage is expected

  • General CFD16 problems that can be handled through a low-order MPS representation [21], requiring many readouts to check entire solution profiles in time evolution
  1. 14Regardless of whether the advantage is exponential
  2. 15Matrix product state
  3. 16Computational fluid dynamics

08 | Architecture enhancement

An on-chip architecture that prepares as many copies of the system
as the number of readouts can trade qubits for execution time.

Suggestion of architecture enhancement

Issues

  • In the time-evolution simulation, the entire computation must be re-executed after a readout at an intermediate time step
➜

Solution suggested

  • Prepare one copy of the system per readout on a single chip and run them simultaneously

An on-chip architecture may have an advantage over parallel or distributed computing because of less communication overhead, shared factories, and direct operations between copies.

09 | Further directions

It is desirable to integrate different quantum technologies into a single platform
to maximize the applicability of quantum computing in real industrial problems.

Directions suggested

01Time crystal [23]

  • Maintaining robust coherent states of qubits for quantum memory

02On-device quantum chip [24]

  • Robust and precise sensing through edge devices17 such as phone, car, and robots [25]

03Quantum reservoir computing

  • Analog approach for measuring, encoding, and preprocessing classical information to quantum computing [26]

Each task is assigned to the best-suited technology within a single platform, connecting through quantum-safe networks18. [27, 28]

  1. 17Photonic devices are candidates for edge devices.
  2. 18Trapped ions for intensive core computing, photons for mobile on-chips, NV for sensing, etc.

11 | QAOA

QAOA is an algorithm dealing with generalized annealing problems [39], including HUBO22.

Workflow

  1. 01Initialize the qubits in a uniform superposition that represents all possible binary solution states of n variables.
  2. 02The cost and mixing layers, parameterized by gamma and beta, alternately act on the qubits p times23.
  3. 03The final state is measured.

Variations

Base QAOA
LR-QAOA24 [41]fixes a linear schedule to set the angle parameters, but it usually requires a large p.
DQAOA [42]divides a large problem into smaller pieces with lower complexity and gathers their results.
DQAOA-GPT [43]uses AI to construct circuits for DQAOA, which belongs to the quantum-classical hybrid approaches.
MA-QAOA [44]uses multi-angle parameters for different qubits.
  1. 22Higher-order Unconstrained Binary Optimization, in which higher-order nonlinear operators are exactly expressed as the combination of two-qubit Pauli-z and CNOT gates [40].
  2. 23The cost layer (gamma) consists of multi-qubit phase shift operators encoding the objective function using multiplications of the Pauli-z operator between coupled variables, thereby generating entanglement, and the mixing layer (beta) consists of one-qubit rotation (Pauli-x) operators searching the solution space. As p goes to infinity, the approximation converges to the desired optimal solution, but a larger p requires more gate operations and longer execution times.
  3. 24Linear Ramp (LR) QAOA fixes a linear schedule for the angle parameters gamma and beta, but it usually requires a large p.

12 | Glossary

Layman’s definition

Quantum mechanics
A branch of physics that usually describes nature at small scales
Quantum
Following the principles of quantum mechanics; originally, the discreteness of energy
Measurement
Extracting information from a system by physical interactions
State
Information about a system specifying the probabilities of all possible measurement outcomes
Quantum state
Superposition of base states, weighted by probability amplitudes

Key Properties

  • Entanglement: a property that the joint states formed through interaction cannot be split into individual independent states
  • Interference: a property that the probability of a measurement outcome becomes the square of the sum of the amplitudes of all contributing components
Qubit
Information unit utilizing two controllable quantum states
Optimization
A process of finding the minimum (or maximum) of objective functions under constraints
